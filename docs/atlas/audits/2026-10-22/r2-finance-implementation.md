# R2 finance implementation — isolated pre-production code

October 22, 2026. Worktree `codex/atlas-r2-marketplace-pilot`, separate from R1 and deployed grading/Ten Kings systems. Production execution remains disabled by default. This specialist used no real provider key or provider action: qualification below uses local persistence and injected HTTP fixtures, including explicitly mocked live-mode responses. A production-capable code path is not live activation or actual Stripe sandbox qualification. Lead-owned account setup and PostgreSQL/runtime qualification have separate evidence.

## Scope and accounting

The implementation covers the financial portion of master IDs 17, 19, 23, 27 and operational portions of 04, 21, 29, 30. All 30 master-plan IDs retain their existing scope. Ownership claims/history remain disabled. No grading math, grading policy, Ten Kings checkout, TKD, shared production runtime or credentials are imported or modified.

`ledger.mjs` uses safe integer USD cents and immutable hash-chained journals. Positive postings are debits; negative postings are credits. Each platform and connected-account book balances independently. Inputs, intermediate arithmetic and cumulative balances reject unsafe integers. `financeBalanceTransactions` gives each provider account/mode/transaction exactly one accounting owner; source, gross, fee, net and currency cannot silently change or be reused by another intent or adjustment.

A captured payment allocates gross money once to seller payable, fee revenue, tax and shipping liabilities. Processing fees are a separate actual expense. A transfer reduces platform cash/payable and adds connected cash/proceeds; it does not create another sale. Only exact connected availability releases proceeds for withdrawal. Payouts reserve specific order allocations, debit cash through payout clearing, then settle allocations after retrieved `paid` evidence. Failed payouts require distinct original debit and returned-funds transactions, restore funds once, and retain any earlier paid completion and its correction.

Refunds have explicit seller/fee/tax/shipping allocations and cumulative component limits. Pending cash debit enters refund clearing; success applies the allocation. A pre-transfer refund reduces seller payable; a post-transfer refund creates seller recovery due. Failed/canceled debited refunds require the separate returned-funds transaction. The unpaid refund obligation remains on hold. If previously collected seller recovery prevents an exact refund-failure correction, reconciliation stops for review rather than creating a negative receivable.

Reversals are distinct intents bounded by the original transfer, outstanding seller debt and unwithdrawn connected proceeds. After payout, unrecovered money stays debt; no available balance or bank debit is invented. Disputes record actual platform debit/fees and explicitly approved seller recovery. A second distinct dispute for a sale or refund during an unresolved dispute requires review. Reinstatement never silently re-transfers money already recovered from a seller.

The pilot conservatively blocks new withdrawals for a seller with any order hold, unresolved recovery, refund or open dispute. Operational account exceptions also block relevant transfers/payouts, including future orders on that account. The UI can distinguish Pending, Available, Reserved, Withdrawal processing, Paid out and Recovery due. No escrow, bank, insurance, guaranteed release date, partial-hold release or spending-wallet promise is made.

## Reducers and asynchronous orchestration

Reducers are synchronous `store.transaction(tx => ...)` functions using `{state,now,emit}`. The enclosing store may return a value or Promise; payment, webhook and reconciliation orchestration awaits both forms. The synthetic provider remains a local SQLite fixture; a separate local provider store can accompany PostgreSQL domain tests.

Existing public finance reducers:

- `preparePayment`, `prepareTransfer`, `prepareRefund`, `prepareReversal`, `preparePayout`.
- `claimDispatch`, `bindFinanceObject`, `markFinanceUnknown`, `reconcileFinance`.
- `reconcileDispute` / `recordDispute`, `recordHold`, `releaseHold`, `sellerBalance`.

The immutable order binds order/reservation/listing/card/seller/buyer, USD, item/fee/seller-net/tax/shipping/total cents, policy version and binding hash. Seller net plus fee must equal item; item plus tax plus shipping must equal total. Finance does not grant ownership or fulfillment authority.

A financial intent moves through PREPARED, DISPATCHING, UNKNOWN, PENDING, SUCCEEDED, FAILED or CANCELED. Only retrieved canceled-payment evidence proving zero received, zero captured and unpaid charge permits `terminalUnpaid:true`. A browser return, failure event, `requires_payment_method`, timeout or absent evidence cannot release a card.

`executeFinanceIntent({store,provider,intentId,enabled:false})` commits one dispatch claim and deterministic idempotency identity before I/O. Any create exception consumes that dispatch. `reconcileFinanceIntent({store,provider,intentId,objectId?})` only retrieves. Transfer/reversal/payout recheck account capabilities; payout refreshes balance and account reconciliation. A new request never substitutes for an unknown original action.

`getPaymentCollection({store,provider,intentId,buyerId,reservationId,enabled:false})` verifies the retained buyer/reservation before requesting a transient payment capability. The provider re-retrieves the original PaymentIntent and checks metadata, amount, currency, account mode, automatic confirmation/capture and collectible status. It returns only `strategy:'stripe-payment-element',paymentIntentId,clientSecret,publishableKey,amountCents,currency`, with canonical retained `intentId,reservationId` appended by the orchestration boundary. The trusted HTTP layer authenticates the buyer and uses no-store responses; neither secret nor onboarding URL belongs in persistence, logs or analytics. Stripe.js confirmation must use the configured application return route; server reconciliation establishes payment truth.

`provider-stripe-sandbox.mjs` preserves `createStripeSandboxProvider({authorizedSandbox:true,secretKey,publishableKey?,platformAccountId,allowedConnectedAccountIds,allowedReturnOrigins?,apiVersion,fetch?,clock?})`. It always uses test authorization and rejects live keys even if a live flag is supplied.

`provider-stripe.mjs` exports:

- `createStripeAuthorization({environment:'PROVIDER_SANDBOX'|'PRODUCTION',platformAccountId,allowedConnectedAccountIds,apiVersion,allowedReturnOrigins,approvalRefs})`: validates and brands an immutable server-only configuration. Serialized/copied browser objects cannot replace that brand.
- `createStripeProvider({authorization,secretKey,publishableKey?,liveExecutionEnabled:false,fetch?,clock?})`: no implicit environment, account or live activation. PRODUCTION requires separate `liveExecutionEnabled:true` and a live key; sandbox requires a test key.
- `STRIPE_PRODUCTION_APPROVAL_REFS`: `activationRef,merchantLiabilityRef,connectControlRef,feePolicyRef,refundDisputePolicyRef,payoutPolicyRef,authorityRef,securityQualificationRef`. The runtime must independently verify these references and its policy/account/authority binding. The brand alone does not certify approvals.

No provider factory reads environment files, searches credentials, retries writes, selects accounts, or permits an API-origin override. Requests target Stripe's API, pin version/account, reject redirects and bound response size/time. Identity is verified with the exact platform account and matching balance mode. All created/retrieved objects, parent charges/transfers, connected transactions, account capabilities, balance evidence, disputes and webhook events must match the retained mode. Production policy expects live evidence; sandbox/synthetic expect test evidence.

Provider methods are `create`, `retrieve`, `getAccount`, `getBalance`, `getIdentity`, `getPaymentCollection`, `createOnboardingLink`, `getAccountSnapshot`, `listBalanceTransactions`, `discover`, `listRelatedObjects`, `retrieveDispute`, and `acceptsAccount`. `getIdentity` returns provider/environment/mode, platform and allowed connected accounts, pinned API version and collection/onboarding configuration flags. Those flags describe configuration, not successful end-to-end qualification.

Onboarding operates only on prebound allowlisted connected accounts. The seller service supplies its authoritative seller-to-account binding and an approved `policyEvidence` binding account/version/reference. Collection fields are explicitly `currently_due` or `eventually_due`; refresh/return URLs must be HTTPS on configured origins. Only a Stripe-hosted single-use account link is returned. No controller, liability model, country, fees or account-creation policy is invented. Stripe-hosted bank collection must be configured for the account model. Raw bank account/routing details are never collected by this package.

`getAccount` now distinguishes Stripe payout eligibility from independent bank ownership verification. Stripe documents `new` as the ordinary external payout-bank status; `validated`/`verified` describe other verification contexts and are not assumed to occur for an external payout account. A destination qualifies only with exact connected-account ownership of the provider object, USD, status `new`, standard payout support, no current/past-due/errors/pending-verification bank requirements, and current parent-account requirements, active transfer capability, enabled payouts and manual schedule. The returned `payoutEligibleExternalAccountIds` contains those IDs; `bankAccounts[].qualification` is `STRIPE_PAYOUT_ELIGIBLE` or `UNAVAILABLE`. `verifiedExternalAccountIds` remains an identical compatibility alias with its historical meaning explicitly restricted to provider payout eligibility. None of these fields proves the seller owns the real bank account; operational bank-verification policy remains a separate activation gate.

## Webhook and recovery handling

`verifyRawWebhook` requires original Buffer bytes, bounded payload/header sizes, constant-time HMAC comparison and bounded timestamp tolerance. Test mode is the default; live mode requires server-set `expectedLivemode:true`. `handleFinanceWebhook` is default-off, retains event ID/raw hash/account/type, rejects collisions, and re-retrieves current provider objects even for duplicates.

Direct payment/refund/transfer/reversal/payout events reconcile original intents. `charge.refunded` lists exact charge refunds; `transfer.reversed` lists exact transfer reversals. Known durable intents reconcile; unowned financial actions become review exceptions. Disputes retrieve the charge and debit/reinstatement transactions. The server supplies `resolveDisputePolicy({payment,evidence})`; absent explicit allocation policy or a warning/no-debit dispute creates a durable review hold without inventing debt. Account/bank/capability events refresh account evidence; restricted/deauthorized accounts freeze relevant funds. Connected balance availability rechecks pending transfer settlement. Unsupported or contradictory events remain operational exceptions; no event-body status directly posts money.

An early dispute can be the first notification of an already captured payment. After exact fresh payment reconciliation, the receipt and durable webhook inbox expose `affectedPaymentIntentId` separately from a direct financial `intentId`; a dispute ID never substitutes for a payment ID. The service settles that original reservation into its PAID order even when the dispute returns REVIEW_REQUIRED, retaining its finance hold. Missing charge/payment references cannot accidentally match an unconfirmed intent with no charge identifier. Duplicate notifications retrieve again without posting a second sale. The lead's fulfillment reducer now atomically refuses PACK/DISPATCH/RELEASE on an unconfirmed/blocked finance order or a matching open platform/connected-account exception. It permits recording delivery observations for parcels already dispatched and does not obstruct payment settlement initialization.

`reconcilePendingFinance({store,provider,intentIds?,limit:100,enabled:false})` is a bounded GET-only worker. It handles pending/unknown actions and transfer availability; explicit IDs can revisit dispatched paid/failed actions. Unknown Stripe IDs use bounded metadata-exact list discovery and require a complete unique result. Incomplete/absent results remain unknown; no second create or new idempotency key occurs. Provider calls and asynchronous stores are awaited. Failures retain sanitized durable exceptions.

`recovery.mjs` now provides the default-off operational composition `createFinanceRecoveryRunner({enabled:false,store,provider,config,assertRuntimeReady})`. The host must explicitly supply all config keys: `environment` (`SYNTHETIC_LOCAL`, `PROVIDER_SANDBOX` or `PRODUCTION`), `platformAccountId`, `connectedAccountIds` (1–32 unique IDs), `approvalRef`, `maxItems` (1–500), `concurrency` (1–8, no larger than maxItems), `maxEvidenceAgeMs` (1–300000) and `maxDurationMs` (1000–300000). The synchronous server readiness port must return exactly true; neither config references nor an asynchronous result authorize execution. Provider identity must match environment, live mode, platform and the exact configured connected-account allowlist. The runner never loads secrets and retains only identity/retrieve/discover/snapshot/inventory capabilities.

Call `await runner.run({runId,signal?})` once per operational pass with a fresh unique run ID. Its durable `financeRecoveryRuns` receipt contains selected original IDs, sanitized per-intent/account results, `affectedPaymentIntentIds`, backlog count, timestamps and completion state. The batch selects only already-dispatched original actions in DISPATCHING/DISPATCHED/UNKNOWN/PENDING, transfers awaiting availability, or verified terminal payments whose reservation/order handoff is unfinished. PREPARED actions are never dispatched. Attempt ordering gives previously unvisited intents priority. Original IDs are retrieved directly or through complete exact metadata discovery, then existing finance reducers reconcile them. The platform and all configured connected accounts are reconciled after the admitted intent batch; no opening, external adjustment or exception release is automated.

A transactional platform/environment lease prevents normal overlapping passes. Repeating a run ID returns its saved receipt, including RUNNING; another ID returns BUSY while the lease is active. After a process interruption, a later pass can expire the old lease and mark that old receipt INTERRUPTED. Any already-admitted old GET can only reconcile its original financial object; it cannot overwrite the newer receipt or release its lease. A canceled signal or elapsed admission deadline stops new intent/account work. The runner awaits all admitted work before returning, so it leaves no detached tasks. `maxDurationMs` is an admission deadline, not a claim to forcibly cancel an in-flight adapter request; the actual Stripe adapter separately bounds network time and inventory pagination.

Completion is `COMPLETED`, `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, `INTERRUPTED` or `ERROR`. COMPLETED means the selected observations/reports completed, not that every provider action is terminal; inspect each intent status and `pendingIntentCount`. Reports, unresolved originals and errors remain durable. A stopped pass can be followed by a new run ID safely because only GET recovery is permitted. Host scheduling, retry cadence, alert delivery, retention and separately approved activation remain unregistered responsibilities of the host runtime.

For each `affectedPaymentIntentId`, the trusted host should use that retained payment's reservation/buyer binding and its existing original-payment reconciliation/settlement service. The runner does not construct an order, bypass finance/dispute holds or infer a buyer identity. Verified terminal-unpaid cancellation is handed off similarly for exact reservation release. If the host crashes before settlement, the durable receipt retains the handoff and a later pass rediscovers the unfinished payment, including one first confirmed during dispute reconciliation.

Six focused recovery tests passed on Node22 with an asynchronous store facade and synthetic provider evidence: disabled/unqualified/mode rejection, original unknown recovery and two-account proof, retained settlement handoff, overlapping bounded workers and cooperative abort, expired lease fencing, missing/incomplete data and redacted provider errors, refusal to dispatch PREPARED actions, and unexplained movement retaining an operational hold. No actual provider calls or scheduler registration occurred.

## Account reconciliation and payout prerequisite

`reconciliation.mjs` provides `recordAccountAdjustment`, `recordAccountReport`, `reconcileAccount`, `recordFinanceException`, `resolveFinanceException` and `reconcileProviderDispute`.

An opening is an explicitly reviewed provider snapshot under a quiescent cutoff, established before that account's first cash journal. Opening cash offsets opening equity, never seller earnings. External movements require exact account/mode/USD transaction evidence and a staff-reviewed EXTERNAL adjustment; their offset is external suspense, never seller availability. The transaction registry prevents double adoption. Live adjustments additionally require explicit live-mode approval. These trusted reducers must never receive provider evidence reconstructed from a customer request.

`reconcileAccount({store,provider,accountId,platformAccountId,reportId,maxEvidenceAgeMs,enabled:false})` brackets a complete balance-transaction inventory with provider snapshots. It compares every known transaction's immutable fields, detects unaccounted/missing/duplicate movements, checks opening rollforward and journal cash, and reports unsupported balance components, changing snapshots, unknown dispatch and open operational exceptions. Known pending intents are listed separately. A retrieval failure stores UNAVAILABLE instead of a fabricated total. Each report ID is unique. Inventory/discovery are bounded; truncated history cannot qualify and must be completed/reviewed operationally.

**Stripe-mode payout preparation and dispatch both require fresh RECONCILED reports for the platform and connected account.** They must match account/mode, current cash-ledger hash and cash amount, and connected provider available balance. The latest sequence wins. The age limit is the retained finance policy's `maxEvidenceAgeMs`. An internal payout reservation does not touch cash and preserves the report; a debit, return, transfer or other cash posting invalidates it. Platform coverage is required because an external refund/dispute can create recovery exposure even while connected funds remain available. Synthetic fixtures are exempt.

The service should reconcile both accounts immediately before a new/prepared payout. Already-dispatched unknown actions must reconcile their original object first. Missing opening evidence, unexplained movement or stale report blocks withdrawal. No automatic adjustment or exception resolution occurs. Staff review is explicit and retains its evidence.
